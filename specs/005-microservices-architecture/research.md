## R18. Memory-first performance model (governs every other decision)

**Decision**:
- **Principles**: read frequently used, safely cacheable data from memory. Persist only what durable business state, recovery, idempotency, reliable delivery, and externally visible guarantees require. Never acknowledge Scan or Complete before its local commit. Combine related writes in one local transaction. Keep remote calls off Scan. Batch Analytics work.
- **Caches**: every cache is a projection of committed rows, updated only after commit and rebuildable after restart. No cache is the sole record of acknowledged state (C-II).
- **Durability settings**: MySQL settings stay unchanged (`innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=1`, `sync_binlog=1`, binary logging on) and are recorded with every benchmark.
- **Infrastructure**: none is added. Redis and Kafka are excluded unless a benchmark later demonstrates a concrete benefit (R23).

**Cost model**: from the retained service-based evidence in [load-client/reports/transaction-optimization/README.md](../../load-client/reports/transaction-optimization/README.md).
- **Commit cost**: on the benchmark machine every durable commit costs about 7.5–7.7 ms at 10 stations, and repeated Scans dominate basket time. At 100 stations, pool queuing and then inventory hot-row contention during Complete dominate. `Innodb_log_waits` stays at 0.
- **Current baseline**: about 4,750 for the default run and about 14,164 for the stress run, both `totalTransactions`.

| Operation | Mandatory durable work (required by the cited requirement) | Optional work removed by this design |
|---|---|---|
| Start | 1 INSERT + commit (FR-018) | Post-insert SELECT; timestamps are generated by the service |
| Scan | 1 guarded UPDATE of the transaction row + 1 INSERT of the accepted-scan row (basket record and outbox) + commit (FR-019, FR-035, FR-040) | `SELECT … FOR UPDATE`, catalog SELECT, line upsert, line-price SELECT, basket `SUM` SELECT, and two autocommit toggles: 10 round trips become 3 |
| Complete T1 | 1 guarded UPDATE + 1 INSERT of the attempt with its line snapshot + commit (C-V: persist before the remote step) | Basket reads (served from cache); child-table inserts |
| Complete T2 | 1 gRPC call. In Inventory: 1 locking SELECT + 1 INSERT of the outcome + 1 UPDATE of stock + commit (FR-026, FR-028) | Claim-then-update double write; per-line statements; child table |
| Complete T3 | 1 guarded UPDATE of the transaction + 1 UPDATE of the attempt + commit (FR-034) | Receipt reads; the receipt is built from cache plus a service timestamp |
| Relay, per batch of up to 500 events | 1 SELECT + 1 gRPC call + 1 batched UPDATE + commit (FR-041) | Per-event statements |
| Analytics ingestion, per batch | Sequence lock + duplicate check + 1 multi-row INSERT with positions + sequence UPDATE + commit (FR-042, FR-043) | A separate materializer commit with one UPDATE per event; repeated name upserts |
| Window publication, every 500 positions | Header + ranks + checkpoint + commit (FR-047) | None |

**Expected trade-off**:
- **Commit count**: commits on the critical path per basket become N + 4 (Start, N Scans, T1, Inventory, T3), against N + 2 in the single-database design. These two extra commits are the mandatory cost of database per service with a persisted Saga. Tracing them back: C-II gives Inventory its own commit, and C-V requires T1 before the remote step and T3 after it.
- **Offset**: the memory-first Scan removes about 7 round trips per Scan. With about 10.5 Scans per basket, that partly offsets the extra commits at 10 stations, and shorter connection-hold times reduce pool queuing at 100 stations.
- **Measurement**: the net effect is measured at checkpoints (R22), not assumed.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Relaxing durability* (`innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit=2`, `sync_binlog=0`, binary logging off, delayed group commit): forbidden.
- *Application-level batching of Scan commits across requests*: delays acknowledgements at 10 stations, and MySQL group commit already batches concurrent commits.
- *Skipping T1*: violates C-V and FR-035 after a crash.

## R1. Building gRPC and Protocol Buffers into the existing plain-Java build

**Decision**: Add one Maven module, `server/contracts/`, that owns the version-controlled `.proto` files. It generates Java stubs with `protoc` and `protoc-gen-grpc-java`, compiles them into `server/out/contracts.jar`, and copies the pinned gRPC runtime dependencies into `server/lib/grpc/`.
- **Generators**: `os-maven-plugin` detects the host OS, and the protobuf Maven plugin downloads the matching `protoc` and gRPC plugin binaries from Maven Central.
- **Rest of the build**: `server/build.sh` runs this module first, then compiles `common` and the three services with `javac` and `jar` as today, adding `out/contracts.jar` and `lib/grpc/*` to their classpaths.
- **Pinning**: one gRPC Java release, via its BOM, and its matching `protoc` are pinned when implementation starts.
- **Generated files**: `server/lib/grpc/` is git-ignored. The first build needs network access; after that, Maven's local cache supports `-o`.

**Rationale**: Maven 3.9.9 and JDK 21 are installed; `protoc` is not. Limiting Maven to the generated-contract artifact keeps the proven build, run, and test scripts. Constitution I allows shared generated stubs that carry no business logic.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Full Maven multi-module build*: rewrites every script and the shared test tree.
- *Vendored gRPC jars plus committed generated sources*: risk of drift from the `.proto` files.
- *Vendored native `protoc`*: platform-specific binaries in the repository.

## R2. gRPC runtime conventions

**Decision**:
- **Transport**: `grpc-netty-shaded`, plaintext on the local host.
- **Channels**: one `ManagedChannel` per target per process, created at startup and reused.
- **Executors**: virtual-thread server executors.
- **Retries**: application-level, reusing idempotency identities.
- **Message sizes**: default 4 MiB limits. The catalog is about 100 KB, and a 500-event batch about 60 KB.
- **Tests**: `grpc-inprocess`.

**Rationale**: Constitution III asks for reused channels, deadlines, and safe retries. Application-level retries respect the response budget.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *OkHttp transport*: less mature server support.
- *Built-in gRPC retry policy*: opaque to budgets and idempotency keys.
- *Asynchronous stubs*: no benefit on virtual threads.

## R3. Database per service: layout and credentials

**Decision**:
- **Layout**: one local MySQL 8 server on port 3307 with three schemas: `cs6510_transaction`, `cs6510_inventory`, and `cs6510_analytics`.
- **Users**: three users, `cs6510_<service>_svc`, each granted only its own schema. A one-time admin bootstrap creates them.
- **No cross-schema references**.
- **Credentials**: each service reads `<SERVICE>_DB_NAME`, `<SERVICE>_DB_USER`, and `<SERVICE>_DB_PASSWORD`, plus the shared `DB_HOST` and `DB_PORT`.
- **Verification**: isolation is tested by expecting MySQL error 1142.

**Rationale**: This is the constitution's permitted local form. Separate credentials make ownership enforceable and testable.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Three MySQL servers*: heavier, no added guarantee.
- *One user*: isolation would not be enforceable.
- *Shared `DB_*` variables*: risk of leaking one service's account into another JVM.

## R4. Product and price replica: durable copy plus in-memory cache

**Decision**:
- **Source**: Inventory keeps a `catalog_meta.catalog_version` UUID that every reset or reseed regenerates, and serves `GetCatalogVersion` and `GetCatalog` from its in-memory catalog cache (R8).
- **Replica**: Transaction keeps a durable `product_replica` table plus a version row, and an immutable in-memory snapshot (SKU to name and price) that Scan reads.
- **Startup**: Transaction serves its last durable replica.
- **Refresh**: a background refresher checks the version every 30 s. When the version changes, it fetches the snapshot, replaces the table in one local transaction, then swaps the in-memory snapshot. The staleness bound is 30 s plus one fetch.
- **Empty replica**: if the replica has never been populated, Scan returns 500 (FR-023), and the loader retries every second.
- **Benchmarks**: the reset procedure restarts Transaction after Inventory is reseeded.

**Rationale**: Scan validates and captures name and price with no SQL and no remote call (FR-006, FR-012). The durable copy keeps Scan available across a Transaction restart during an Inventory outage.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Per-scan gRPC*: violates FR-012.
- *Memory-only replica*: Scan fails after a restart during an outage.
- *Inventory pushes changes*: no catalog-change operation justifies it.

## R5. Checkout Saga: steps, identifiers, and forward recovery

**Decision**: A Transaction-orchestrated Saga with forward recovery after the pivot.

| Step | Owner | Local transaction (statements) | Compensation |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 Begin attempt | Transaction | Guarded UPDATE that sets `attempt_count = n` and `active_attempt_number = n` where the row is OPEN, has no active attempt, and still has the cached `item_count` and `attempt_count` (`n − 1`). INSERT `checkout_attempt` with its canonical `lines` snapshot and totals. Commit. | Reopen: settle the attempt as REJECTED and clear the active attempt |
| T2 Apply stock effect (pivot) | Inventory | `ApplyCheckout`: one locking SELECT, one INSERT of the outcome, one UPDATE of stock if applied, commit (R8) | Reserved stock restoration; never invoked by normal checkout |
| T3 Record completion | Transaction | Guarded UPDATE to COMPLETED where `active_attempt_number = n`, plus UPDATE of the attempt to COMPLETED, commit | None: retriable and cannot fail definitively. Principle V of constitution v5.1.0 permits this for a step after a forward-recovery pivot. |

- **Attempt identifier**: `(transaction_id, attempt_number)`.
- **Reconciliation**: an idempotent replay of `ApplyCheckout`. `GetCheckoutOutcome` serves diagnostics and validation.
- **Recovery**: an event-driven worker (R7) resumes PENDING attempts that no live request owns, with backoff and no retry limit. Its work comes from three sources:
  - an in-memory schedule fed by request handovers and reconcile entries;
  - a startup scan;
  - a bounded, indexed safety scan every 30 s.
- **Integrity faults**: `FAILED_PRECONDITION` and `INVALID_ARGUMENT` keep the attempt pending with an alarm.
- **Receipts**: built from the cached snapshot and a service-generated `completed_at`, so no read follows the T3 commit.

**Rationale**: This implements FR-030 and FR-032 with the minimum statements per step. The guarded UPDATE makes correctness independent of the cache (R19).

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Backward recovery*: rejected in clarification.
- *Hold then confirm*: an extra step with no benefit under forward recovery.
- *A child table for attempt lines*: extra inserts.
- *Per-line Inventory calls*: violate FR-026.

## R19. Transaction state cache with guarded writes

**Decision**: Transaction keeps a bounded in-memory map of OPEN transactions. Each entry holds:
- `status`, `item_count`, `total_amount`, `attempt_count`, and `active_attempt_number`;
- `lines`, mapping each SKU to its quantity, captured price, and name.

**Locks**: requests are serialized per transaction by `TransactionLocks`, which is independent of the cache entries.
- It is a fixed array of 4,096 striped `ReentrantLock`s, indexed by the hash of the transaction ID.
- Stripes are never created, evicted, or replaced, so evicting or invalidating an entry can never create a second lock for the same transaction.
- A request acquires its stripe before it reads the entry, and never uses an entry obtained without the stripe.
- Code holds at most one stripe at a time, and never holds one across a remote call or a wait.
- **Collisions**: two transactions that share a stripe affect each other's performance only. One waits for the other's short local transaction, but no deadlock is possible, and the database guards decide every outcome. Lock wait time, hold time, contention, and collisions are recorded (R21), and a test reruns a concurrent workload with a single stripe to show identical outcomes.

Rules:
1. **Updated only after commit**: an entry changes only after a successful commit.
   - **Unknown commit outcome**: any exception after the commit was attempted, including a `commitOutcomeUnknown` failure, invalidates the entry before the stripe is released. The next request for that transaction reloads it from the database before any further mutation, which reconciles the cache with whatever was committed.
   - **Ambiguous T1**: an ambiguous T1 commit also queues a reconcile entry for the recovery worker (R7), which settles the attempt if it exists and drops the entry if it does not.
2. **Guarded writes**: every write to the `transaction` row is a conditional UPDATE that verifies the expected state from the cache (`status`, `active_attempt_number`, `item_count`, and, for T1, `attempt_count`). Zero affected rows means the cache disagrees with the database: the request rolls back, reloads from the database, and re-evaluates. That outcome can be 404, 409, a FR-035 wait, or a retry.
3. **Errors confirmed**: success paths are protected by the guarded write. Error responses (404 or 409 on Scan or Complete) are confirmed against the database before they are sent, so a stale entry can never change validation precedence (FR-020, FR-025).
4. **Cold path**: on a cache miss, or after a restart, the request reads the `transaction` row plus `accepted_scan` grouped by SKU, then caches the result.
5. **Eviction**: COMPLETED transactions leave the cache. An LRU cap applies (`TRANSACTION_CACHE_MAX_ENTRIES`, default 100,000), and setting it to 0 disables the cache, which allows an A/B measurement and provides a fallback.
6. **Lookup**: reads the database. It is not on the hot path, and reading the database removes any staleness question.
7. **Single writer**: Transaction is the only writer of its schema (C-II). Correctness does not depend on this assumption, because a second writer would only cause guarded-write misses.
8. **Multiple instances**: the deployment runs one Transaction instance, but no guarantee may depend on that.
   - The cache, the locks, the settlement registry, and the recovery schedule (R7) are process-local optimizations, not distributed coordination.
   - Every correctness guarantee must hold when several instances concurrently scan, complete, or recover the same transaction. Database guards and Inventory's idempotency are the authority.
9. **Verification**: concurrency tests cover the cache's failure modes:
   - stale entries;
   - eviction while requests for the same transaction are in flight;
   - stripe collisions;
   - ambiguous commits;
   - two independent Transaction instances over one schema, scanning, completing, and recovering the same transactions.

**Rationale**: This removes the `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` and every read from the warm Scan and Complete paths (R18). Concurrent Scan and Complete still serialize on the database row lock that the guarded UPDATE takes, which preserves the tested "one serial outcome" property and FR-035.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *No cache*: a cold path on every request adds two reads per Scan.
- *A cache without guarded writes*: unsafe.
- *A process-wide lock*: needlessly serializes unrelated transactions.
- *A lock stored in each cache entry*: evicting an entry while a request holds its lock lets the next request create a second lock for the same transaction.
- *A reference-counted lock map*: gives the same guarantee as striping, with more code. Striping's only cost is that unrelated transactions sharing a stripe occasionally wait for one short local transaction.

## R6. Deadlines, response budget, and retries

**Decision**:

| Call or wait | Bound | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| Complete response budget | 8 s from handler entry | After it, return 500; the attempt stays pending |
| `ApplyCheckout` per call | 2 s | Within the budget, retry after 50, 100, 200, and 400 ms on uncertain statuses. Then the recovery worker uses backoff from 100 ms to 5 s, indefinitely |
| Bounded wait (FR-035) | The remaining budget | Then 500, with no change |
| `GetCatalogVersion` and `GetCatalog` | 2 s and 5 s | Every 30 s, at startup, and on a version change; backoff on failure |
| `DeliverScanEvents` | 5 s | Backoff from 100 ms to 5 s; re-send |
| `RestoreCheckout` (reserved) | 2 s | When used, retried until confirmed |

- **Uncertain statuses**: `UNAVAILABLE`, `DEADLINE_EXCEEDED`, `ABORTED`, `RESOURCE_EXHAUSTED`, `CANCELLED`, `UNKNOWN`, and `INTERNAL`.
- **Definitive statuses**: `OK` with an outcome, `INVALID_ARGUMENT`, and `FAILED_PRECONDITION`. The last two are faults: the attempt keeps pending and raises an alarm.

**Rationale**: The budget plus handler overhead stays below the Load Client's 10 s timeout and the gateway's 30 s. A healthy `ApplyCheckout` takes a few milliseconds, so background completions should not occur under healthy load (FR-058).

**Alternatives considered**:
- *One long deadline*: one slow call would consume the whole budget.
- *A short budget*: more background completions under stress.

## R7. Bounded waiting without held connections or locks

**Decision**:
- **Pending detection**: from the cache entry. A cache miss reads the row once.
- **Waiting is event-driven**:
  - The waiter gets or creates the attempt's `SettlementRegistry` future while holding the transaction's stripe lock, then releases the lock and waits.
  - The orchestrator or recovery worker completes the future under the same stripe lock after the settlement commit. A settlement in the same process therefore wakes the waiter without any database read, and no wake-up can be lost.
- **Database checks**: a waiter reads the database only once per `CHECKOUT_WAIT_BACKSTOP_MS` (1,000 ms) as a backstop, which catches settlements made elsewhere, and once at the bound. Each check borrows a connection only for its read.
- **Recovery is event-driven too**:
  - The worker takes due attempts from an in-memory `RecoverySchedule`. Request handovers (budget expiry) and reconcile entries (ambiguous T1 commits) feed it, as do the worker's own backoff retries.
  - A paginated startup scan rebuilds the schedule, because nothing is owned after a restart.
  - A safety scan runs every `CHECKOUT_RECOVERY_SCAN_SECONDS` (30): one indexed query on `(state, next_retry_at)` with `LIMIT 100`, repeated only while pages are full.
  - T1 sets `next_retry_at` to the end of the request's budget, so scans leave live attempts alone.
- **Resources**: no database connection, lock, or transaction is held while waiting. The stripe lock is released before waiting, and T1 and T3 never span the RPC.
- **Process-local only**: the registry and the schedule speed up a single process; they do not coordinate processes.
  - A waiter learns of another instance's settlement through its database backstop, then completes and removes its local future.
  - Another instance's worker may replay an attempt that this process owns, and Inventory's idempotency and the guarded writes settle it once.
  - The worker reads an attempt by key before replaying it, and drops attempts that are already settled or absent.
  - A request whose own attempt was settled by another actor records its `client_outcome` before answering (R9).
- **After settlement**: the request restarts against the settled state. COMPLETED gives 409 `TRANSACTION_NOT_OPEN`; OPEN means normal processing.
- **At the bound**: 500, with no change.
- **Lookup**: never waits.

**Rationale**: This satisfies FR-033 and FR-035 and the no-held-resources constraint. Waits end as soon as the attempt settles. Healthy waits and an idle recovery worker issue no database reads, and every remaining query is indexed and bounded. Crash recovery still holds, because the durable `next_retry_at` and the startup scan rebuild everything the in-memory schedule held.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Holding the row lock across the RPC*: forbidden.
- *Polling only* (a 250 ms wait re-check and a 500 ms recovery poll): slower to react, and adds reads that hold pooled connections under load.
- *Rejecting immediately*: rejected in clarification.

## R8. Inventory operations: atomic, idempotent, minimal statements; catalog cache

**Decision**: `ApplyCheckout` runs as one Inventory local transaction at READ COMMITTED (R20):
1. `SELECT sku, stock_quantity FROM inventory WHERE sku IN (…) ORDER BY sku FOR UPDATE` locks every line's row in SKU order.
2. The outcome is computed in memory:
   - unknown SKU: `INVALID_REQUEST` with the first such SKU;
   - any shortfall: `INSUFFICIENT_STOCK` with the first such SKU in SKU order;
   - otherwise `APPLIED`.
3. INSERT `checkout_operation` with the outcome and the canonical `lines` text.
4. If APPLIED, one `UPDATE inventory SET stock_quantity = CASE sku … END WHERE sku IN (…)`.
5. Commit.

Duplicates, conflicts, and the reserved operation:
- **Duplicates**: a duplicate request blocks on the same row locks. Its INSERT then hits the primary key, so it rolls back, reads the recorded outcome, and returns it with `replayed = true`. If the canonical `lines` differ, it returns `FAILED_PRECONDITION`.
- **Contention**: deadlocks and lock-wait timeouts are retried internally. Exhaustion returns `ABORTED`, never insufficient stock (FR-026).
- **Defense in depth**: a unique stored generated column, `applied_transaction_id`, enforces at most one effective deduction per transaction (FR-029).
- **Reserved `RestoreCheckout`**: one transaction that locks the operation row:
  - APPLIED: add the lines back and mark RESTORED;
  - RESTORED: already restored;
  - REJECTED: nothing to restore;
  - absent: insert a BLOCKED tombstone.
- **Operational use**: none in this iteration (FR-032).
- **Multiple instances** (Constitution II, v5.1.0): every Inventory guarantee rests on row locks, the operation primary key, the unique `applied_transaction_id`, and the `CHECK ≥ 0` constraint. Concurrent Inventory instances therefore apply each attempt once and never oversell. The catalog cache is a read-only, versioned projection.

Catalog cache:
- Inventory caches the catalog snapshot and version in memory. `GetCatalog` and `GET /items` are served from it.
- The version is re-read from `catalog_meta` on each `GetCatalogVersion`, and on a change the cache reloads.
- Stock is never cached. Every apply and every low-stock query reads stock rows from the database.

**Rationale**: Four round trips per apply, independent of basket size. SKU-ordered locks give deadlock-resistant, all-or-nothing deductions, as today. Rejections are recorded atomically (FR-028).

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Claim-then-update*: two writes to the operation row.
- *One conditional `UPDATE … WHERE stock >= ?` per line*: N statements per apply.
- *In-memory stock counters*: forbidden as the source of truth.
- *Child tables for lines*: extra inserts.

## R9. Counting and minimizing background completions

**Decision**: `checkout_attempt.client_outcome` records the server's response for the attempt's own Complete request:
- `NONE` until decided;
- `SUCCESS`, written in the T3 UPDATE;
- `REJECTED`, written in the reopen UPDATE;
- `PENDING_ERROR`, committed before a pending 500 is sent.

All of these are written in statements that run anyway, with two exceptions:
- `PENDING_ERROR` is written only on the error path.
- When another actor settled the request's own attempt, the request writes `SUCCESS` or `REJECTED` with a guarded UPDATE (`client_outcome = 'NONE'`) before answering. The other actor is the recovery worker of this or another instance, after a race. If that write fails, the request answers 500 instead, so the counts stay exact under multiple instances.

Counts:
- *background completions*: COMPLETED attempts with `client_outcome = PENDING_ERROR`;
- *completions without a recorded response*: COMPLETED with `NONE`;
- *remaining difference*: durable COMPLETED transactions minus `totalTransactions`.

The benchmark harness (R21) records these next to each report. Minimization comes from R6, R16, and R20. The default run must show zero (FR-058, SC-010).

**Rationale**: Only the server knows whether it answered 500 while an attempt was pending. Recording it costs nothing on successful checkouts.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Logs*: not durable.
- *Changing the Load Client*: prohibited.

## R10. Accepted-scan log, transactional outbox, and push relay

**Decision**:
- **One table serves three roles**: Transaction's `accepted_scan` table is the durable scan log, the source of the basket, and the outbox.
- **Scan write**: Scan inserts one row (`scan_id` auto-increment; `(transaction_id, scan_sequence)`; SKU; captured name and price; `accepted_at`; null `delivered_at`) in the same local transaction as the guarded transaction UPDATE.
- **Basket lines**: there is no separate line table. Lines are derived as quantity = count per SKU, plus the captured price and name, which are identical across a transaction's rows for that SKU. They are kept in the cache and rebuilt from these rows after a restart.
- **Relay**: a single relay thread selects up to 500 rows with `delivered_at IS NULL` ordered by `scan_id`, calls `DeliverScanEvents`, and on `OK` sets `delivered_at` with one statement over exactly those IDs, then commits.
- **Failures**: back off from 100 ms to 5 s.
- **Idle polling**: 50 ms.
- **Retention**: delivered rows are kept, because they remain the basket and audit record.
- **Multiple instances**: relays in two Transaction instances may select and deliver the same rows. Analytics' deduplication counts each scan once, and marking a row delivered twice is harmless.

**Rationale**: Removing the line table removes one write and one round trip from every Scan. At-least-once delivery without a cursor never skips a late commit, and one relay gives natural backpressure.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Keeping `transaction_line`*: a redundant write per Scan.
- *A cursor or watermark relay*: either skips late commits or needs fragile in-flight tracking (R23).
- *Analytics pulls*: more RPCs.
- *A message broker*: prohibited without need.
- *Deleting rows after delivery*: loses the basket and audit record.

## R11. Analytics ingestion assigns positions; ordering rule

**Decision**:
- **Event identity**: `(transaction_id, scan_sequence)`.
- **Ingestion**: one Analytics local transaction per batch, at READ COMMITTED:
  1. Lock `scan_sequence` with `FOR UPDATE`.
  2. Select which identities of the batch already exist, with one tuple-IN query on the unique key.
  3. Drop duplicates, including duplicates inside the batch.
  4. Assign `last_position + 1 …` in batch order.
  5. One multi-row INSERT into `accepted_scan`, which has `position` as its primary key.
  6. Upsert `product_name` only for names not already cached (R12).
  7. UPDATE `scan_sequence` and commit, then return `stored` and `duplicates`.
- **Ordering rule**: positions follow the order in which Analytics durably accepts first-seen events: batch order, with batches serialized by the sequence lock. They need not match Transaction's commit order, acceptance time, or delivery order (FR-043).
- **Materializer removed**: the separate SQL Materializer step and its NULL-position pending state go away. Its algorithm (lock the singleton, assign consecutive positions, advance the checkpoint in the same commit) moves into ingestion.
- **Downstream**: `AcceptedScanPoller`, prewarm, and the Window, Ranking, and Persistence stages read and process positions exactly as in feature 004.
- **Visibility delay**: the relay's 50 ms and the poller's 100 ms idle polls give a target under 5 s at p99 when healthy, and a ceiling of 60 s after load stops.
- **Multiple instances**: concurrent Analytics instances cannot reassign or duplicate positions or windows.
  - Ingestion serializes on the `scan_sequence` row lock and deduplicates on the unique event identity.
  - Window publication locks the checkpoint row, accepts only the next boundary, and treats an already-published boundary as a verified no-op.
  - The name map and the window ring are process-local projections.

**Rationale**: About one statement and one commit per batch replace the materializer's one UPDATE per event plus a separate commit. This cuts Analytics' write load on the MySQL server it shares with Transaction, which matters because commit fsync dominates (R18). Gaplessness, immutability, and deduplication are preserved by construction.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Keeping the separate materializer*: an extra UPDATE per event and an extra commit.
- *Source-assigned global sequence*: the commit-order gap problem.
- *Positions without a sequence lock*: gaps under concurrency.

## R12. Product names for popular items

**Decision**:
- **Event content**: events carry the product name captured at scan time.
- **Name table**: Analytics keeps a `product_name (sku, name)` table plus an in-memory map, loaded at startup with at most 2,000 rows. Ingestion upserts only names that are absent or changed in the map, so after warm-up no name writes occur.
- **Popular items**: `popular_item.sku` references `product_name`, and `JdbcPopularWindowStore.readRanks` joins `product_name`.

**Rationale**: No catalog dependency in Analytics, and redundant writes are removed.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Replicating Inventory's catalog into Analytics*: an extra gRPC dependency.
- *An upsert every batch*: redundant writes.

## R13. Independent resets, seeding, and invariant validation

**Decision**:
- **Bootstrap**: `db/bootstrap.ps1`, a one-time admin step, creates the schemas, users, and grants.
- **Reset**: `db/reset-service.ps1 -Service <name>` runs `db/<service>/schema.sql`, plus `seed.sql` for Inventory, with that service's own credentials. The seed reproduces today's deterministic catalog: 2,000 SKUs, price `round(0.5 + (n mod 47) * 0.35, 2)`, 10,000 units, threshold 50, and a new `catalog_version`.
- **Baseline**: `db/reset-all.ps1` resets Inventory, then Transaction, then Analytics.
- **Validation**: `db/validate-invariants.ps1` runs each `db/<service>/validate.sql` with that service's credentials, then reconciles exported aggregates in the script. It checks that:
  - per SKU, sold units equal the quantities from COMPLETED transactions' accepted-scan rows;
  - the Inventory APPLIED set equals the set of COMPLETED attempts;
  - Analytics' event count equals Transaction's delivered `accepted_scan` rows, with matching per-transaction counts;
  - all of a transaction's rows for one SKU carry one price;
  - windows and ranks match a recomputation inside Analytics;
  - no attempts are PENDING;
  - and it prints the FR-054 counts.
- **Old scripts**: the single-database scripts are retired, and Git history keeps them.

**Rationale**: No service writes another's data, and cross-service checks use each owner's credentials.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *A root-run reset*: violates ownership.
- *A cross-schema auditor account*: blurs the isolation evidence.

## R20. JDBC session and driver tuning

**Decision**:
- **Manual-commit connections**: pooled connections are created in manual-commit mode with their isolation level set once, READ COMMITTED for all three services. A shared helper borrows a connection, runs one unit of work, commits or rolls back, and releases it, so no store toggles autocommit or isolation per transaction. This removes two `SET autocommit` round trips from every transaction.
- **Connector/J flags** in each service's JDBC URL:
  - `useLocalSessionState=true` and `useLocalTransactionState=true`;
  - `cachePrepStmts=true`, `prepStmtCacheSize=256`, `prepStmtCacheSqlLimit=2048`;
  - `rewriteBatchedStatements=true`, for multi-row inserts and the relay's batched update.
- **To measure**: `useServerPrepStmts=true` is A/B-measured at checkpoint CP3 and kept only if it helps.
- **Server settings**: no MySQL server setting changes.
- **Locking safety**: every correctness-critical read is a locking read (`FOR UPDATE`), or is protected by a guarded write (R19), so READ COMMITTED is safe. It also avoids gap-lock contention on inserts.

**Rationale**: These are simple, measurable reductions in per-transaction round trips and lock footprint. The existing code toggles autocommit twice per transaction, and the materializer also changed isolation twice per batch.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Keeping per-transaction toggles*: wasted round trips.
- *Multi-statement batches* (`allowMultiQueries`): harder error handling, small extra gain.
- *Stored procedures*: business rules would leave the service code.

## R14. External errors and the gateway

**Decision**:
- **Gateway**: keep `deploy/nginx.conf` with the seven routes, upstreams 8081, 8082, and 8083, `error_page 504 =502`, a 1 s connect timeout, and a 30 s read timeout. gRPC ports are never routed.
- **Error mapping**: handlers keep `ApiErrorMapper`. Every pending, unavailable, or unexpected condition maps to `500 INTERNAL_ERROR` (FR-017). New internal failure types are deliberately unmapped runtime failures, and a mapping test asserts that they never produce a 4xx.

**Rationale**: No public status or code is added.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *503 with a new code*: rejected in clarification.

## R16. Capacity: pools, executors, and MySQL connections

**Decision**:

| Service | Initial pool | Maximum pool |
|---|---:|---:|
| Transaction | 32 | 64 (the retained configuration) |
| Inventory | 16 | 48 (it now writes on every Complete) |
| Analytics | 10 | 10 |

## R21. Instrumentation and benchmark harness

**Decision**: A dependency-free `common/metrics` package replaces `ScanProfiler`.
- **Recording**: named counters and fixed log-bucket latency histograms (50 µs to 30 s) built on `LongAdder`. With `PERF_METRICS=true` (default `false`), each service emits a one-line JSON summary every `PERF_METRICS_INTERVAL_SECONDS` (default 10) and at shutdown. Recording cost is a few nanoseconds; there are no locks on hot paths.

| Area | Measures |
|---|---|
| Transaction | Server-side latency for Start, Scan, Complete, and Lookup; Scan database time; `TransactionLocks` wait and hold time, contention, and stripe collisions; T1 and T3 time; ambiguous commits; `ApplyCheckout` client latency and status counts; budget expiries; FR-035 waits, with signalled wake-ups, database checks, and timeouts; recovery scan queries, rows read, and schedule size; pool borrow wait, hold time, and occupancy; cache hits, misses, invalidations, and guarded-write misses; relay batch size, latency, and idle polls; outbox backlog |
| Inventory | `ApplyCheckout` server latency; locking-SELECT duration (lock contention); commit time; deadlock and lock-wait retries; outcomes; pool borrow wait and hold time |
| Analytics | Ingestion batch size, duplicates, latency, and commit time; poller lag and idle polls; queue depths; window publication latency; visibility delay; pool borrow wait and hold time |

- **Harness**: `server/benchmark-microservices.ps1`, modeled on the existing `benchmark-scan.ps1`. In order, it:
  1. checks ports;
  2. runs `db/reset-all.ps1`;
  3. starts the three services with `PERF_METRICS=true` and the gateway;
  4. runs the unchanged Load Client with exactly the default or stress command, or a `smoke` variant for checkpoints;
  5. samples every 2 s: outbox backlog, pending attempts, Analytics unpublished boundaries, and `SHOW GLOBAL STATUS` (`Innodb_row_lock_waits`, `Innodb_row_lock_time`, `Com_commit`, `Innodb_log_waits`);
  6. waits for zero pending work;
  7. runs `db/validate-invariants.ps1`;
  8. records the MySQL durability variables (`innodb_flush_log_at_trx_commit`, `sync_binlog`, `log_bin`);
  9. writes `evidence.json` beside a byte-identical copy of the client report.

**Rationale**: These are exactly the measurements requested: Scan and Complete p95/p99, pool wait, inventory lock contention, gRPC latency, and analytics backlog. Mandatory durability costs are kept separate from optional costs, and the evidence proves that durability was not weakened.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Micrometer, Prometheus, or JFR dashboards*: extra dependencies or tooling; JFR remains available ad hoc.
- *Client-side metrics only*: they cannot locate costs.

## R22. Performance checkpoints and regression tests

**Decision**:

| Checkpoint | When | Runs | Purpose |
|---|---|---|---|
| CP0 | Before any code change | Existing `benchmark-scan.ps1`: default ×3, stress ×2, on the current implementation | Attribution reference during implementation |
| CP1 | Core checkout path complete: Inventory + Transaction memory-first Scan, Saga, and cache; Analytics may still be down | `benchmark-microservices.ps1 -Workload default` ×2 | Attribution: the commit-count trade-off |
| CP2 | Full system: relay and ingestion | Default ×2, stress ×1 | Attribution: Analytics load, backlog, recovery and wait cost, idle reads |
| CP3 | Tuning A/B: JDBC flags, server prepared statements, pool sizes, statement-budget alternatives | Interleaved A/B pairs per variant on its relevant workloads (`benchmark-compare.ps1`): one screening pair, three confirmation pairs only for a promising variant | Keep only meaningful measured wins |
| CP4 | Final, after the full suite passes | Exactly `java -cp out Main` and `java -cp out Main --stations=100 --duration=120`, each after its own reset, interleaved with fresh CP0 runs from a worktree of the baseline commit: 3 default pairs and 3 stress pairs | The final comparison, and the official evidence (FR-055): the first CP4 run of each workload, designated in advance |

- **Checkpoint record**: each checkpoint records results in `load-client/reports/microservices-architecture/checkpoints.md`.
- **Final comparison**: only the interleaved CP0 and CP4 runs compare the designs. The CP1–CP3 runs ran on other days and at other stages, so they only attribute costs.
- **Claims**: every checkpoint reports the actual client-observed `totalTransactions` and compares it with the CP0 baseline of the same workload. A performance improvement is claimed only from that measurement. Fewer statements and lower latency explain results but never establish an improvement on their own.
- **Regression guideline**: a `totalTransactions` drop of more than 5% against the previous checkpoint, without a correctness reason, is investigated before the next stage. This is not a hard gate, because run variance is about 1–2% at default.
- **A/B decision rule** (`benchmark-compare.ps1`; streamlined 2026-10-22, see below):
  - **Goal**: find meaningful gains in `totalTransactions`, not resolve 1–3% differences. Priority order: correctness, then `totalTransactions`, then p99 latency, then resource use.
  - **Equivalence**: variants differ only in the knob under test. Every run uses all three services, the exact Load Client command, its own reset, and a passing validator. A run that fails validation or is not comparable rejects the variant, whatever its speed.
  - **Order**: pairs alternate their order (A B, B A, …) to cancel drift.
  - **Change**: Δ is the variant's `totalTransactions` median minus the incumbent's, as a percentage of the incumbent's. Differences within ±3% are practically equivalent.
  - **Relevant workloads**: a variant is measured only on the workloads where its knob can realistically change the result. Pool sizes are measured under the 100-station stress workload, because at 10 stations the peak pool occupancy (about 12 Transaction and 6 Inventory connections at CP1–CP2) stays below every candidate's initial pool size. Other knobs are measured on both workloads.
  - **Screening**: one interleaved pair per relevant workload.
    - Δ ≥ +5%: **promising**, so the variant goes to confirmation on that workload.
    - Δ ≤ −3%: **regression**; the variant is rejected.
    - Otherwise: **no demonstrated improvement**; the variant is rejected without further pairs.
  - **Confirmation**: three fresh interleaved pairs on each workload where the variant was promising. The screening pair is reported but not counted, because selecting on it would bias the result upward. There is no extension beyond three pairs.
    - **Improvement**: Δ ≥ +3% and the variant ahead in at least two of the three pairs.
    - Otherwise: **no demonstrated improvement** (or **regression** at Δ ≤ −3%); the variant is rejected.
  - **Regression check**: a confirmed variant also needs at least one default-workload pair, unless the default workload was already measured. It is rejected on any correctness failure, a Δ ≤ −3% on any measured workload, or a Scan or Complete p99 median more than 10% above the incumbent's on any measured workload.
  - **Resource use** (pool wait, lock waits, commits per transaction) is reported to explain results. It never retains a variant on its own.
  - **Confidence**: one pair cannot separate differences under about 3–5% from run-to-run noise, and three pairs only confirm a clear effect. Records state the number of pairs, the medians, the ranges, and the per-pair differences, and claim no statistical significance.
  - **Scope**: variants that cannot plausibly change `totalTransactions` meaningfully are not run. The cache-off attribution run (R19) is skipped unless a correctness issue or a major regression needs it.
  - **Revision**: until 2026-10-22, close results extended to five pairs and were decided by a one-sided Mann–Whitney U test. The four CP3 comparisons completed under that rule (server prepared statements and the 10/32 Inventory pool, each on both workloads) stand as recorded. Neither variant screened at +5% or more, so both would also be rejected under the streamlined rule.

Automated performance regression tests live in `server/tests/performance/`. They use counting and fault-injecting connection decorators, injected through a test-only hook on `ConnectionPool`. They have two kinds of assertion.

**Strict correctness and durability assertions** are exact, gating, and never relaxed:
- **Acknowledgement after commit**: Scan and Complete respond only after their commits return. Analytics replies OK only after its ingestion commit. The relay sets `delivered_at` only after OK.
- **Shared commits**: these writes share one commit:
  - the guarded UPDATE and the `accepted_scan` INSERT;
  - Inventory's outcome row and stock UPDATE;
  - Analytics' events, positions, and sequence.
- **Remote calls**: no remote call on Start or Scan, and exactly one `ApplyCheckout` per warm Complete.
- **Held resources**: no pooled connection or row lock is held during `ApplyCheckout` or during an FR-035 wait, shown by pool occupancy and `FOR UPDATE NOWAIT` with a blocking fake Inventory.
- **Cache safety**: a stale, evicted, or missing entry still yields the correct outcome through the guarded write; error responses are confirmed against the database; and an ambiguous commit is reconciled from the database before the next mutation.
- **Restart rebuilds**: every cache rebuilds after a restart.
- **Architecture**: no Redis or Kafka dependency, no `SET GLOBAL` or durability change in code, and the JDBC URL flags are present.

**Statement budgets** are measured regression guards, not exact requirements:
- **Location**: ceilings live in the versioned file `server/tests/performance/statement-budgets.properties`. Each ceiling is stored with its design value, and tests fail when a count exceeds its ceiling.
- **Raising a ceiling**: allowed only with an A/B record in `checkpoints.md`. The record must show that the alternative raises `totalTransactions` beyond noise, without worse p99 latency or lock contention. A self-check test enforces the evidence label.
- **Lowering a ceiling**: needs no evidence.

The initial design budgets are:

| Area | Design budget (initial ceiling) |
|---|---|
| Warm Scan | 2 statements + 1 commit; no SELECT; no autocommit or isolation calls |
| Start | 1 statement + 1 commit; no SELECT |
| Cold Scan | at most 2 extra reads, once per load |
| Warm Complete | T1: 2 + commit. T3: 2 + commit. Reopen: 2 + commit. No reads after T3 |
| Inventory apply | APPLIED: 3 + commit. Rejected: 2 + commit. Independent of basket size |
| Analytics ingestion | At most 5 statements + 1 commit per batch, independent of batch size; no name writes once names are cached |
| Relay | 1 SELECT + 1 UPDATE + 1 commit per batch |

A non-gating `-Workload smoke` harness run, of 20 s, is part of each checkpoint.

**Rationale**:
- Strict assertions protect correctness and durability deterministically. Budgets catch accidental round trips without freezing the design when a measured alternative is faster.
- Interleaved A/B pairs cancel drift. One screening pair finds effects of about 5% or more, which are the only ones worth keeping a variant for, and three fresh pairs confirm them. Effects of 1–3% are treated as no demonstrated improvement instead of being resolved statistically.
- Benchmark checkpoints attribute costs stage by stage.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Timing-threshold unit tests*: flaky.
- *Exact, immutable statement counts*: would block a demonstrably faster alternative that adds a statement, for example one that shortens lock hold time.
- *Single runs per variant*: cannot separate a 2–3% effect from noise. Accepted for screening only, because a 2–3% effect is not worth keeping a variant for.
- *Extending close results to five pairs with a significance test* (the rule until 2026-10-22): about 1.5–2 hours per variant to resolve differences too small to matter.
- *Measuring only at the end*: cannot attribute regressions.

## R15. Testing and fault injection

**Decision**:
- **Existing suites**: compatible tests are kept unchanged, and the rest are migrated with equivalent assertions (FR-057).
  - **Ledger**: `server/tests/COVERAGE-LEDGER.md` records each legacy test method, its replacement, and the gate that verified it. An assertion of behavior that the spec replaces is recorded as superseded, with its governing requirement. A ledger test enforces the record, and each story's gate fails while an assertion it owns lacks a passing equivalent.
  - **Parking and deletion**: a legacy file is parked only by the task that breaks it, and deleted only after verified parity.
  - **Helpers**: disposable-database helpers are split per schema (`TransactionTestSchema`, `InventoryTestSchema`, `AnalyticsTestSchema`), with disposable users for isolation tests. The names avoid the legacy `AnalyticsDatabase` and `CheckoutDatabase` helpers, which remain until their users are migrated.
- **gRPC contract tests** (`tests/rpc`): in-process and real-port servers.
- **Fault injection**: test-only hooks named in the contracts, inert unless the JVM property `selfcheckout.faults` lists them, with actions `halt`, `delay:<ms>`, and `throw`.
- **Process-level crash tests**: start service JVMs against disposable schemas.
- **Direct restoration tests**: run against Inventory and are reported in their own class (SC-012).
- **Performance regression tests**: R22.

**Rationale**: Faults are reproducible, restarts are real, and direct Inventory tests stay distinct from end-to-end Saga tests.

**Alternatives considered**:
- *Network proxies*: extra tooling.
- *Mocking JDBC*: misses real locking semantics.

## R17. Constitution treatment

**Decision**: the plan is checked against constitution v5.1.0, adopted on 2026-10-20 from the reviewed and approved [amendment proposal](constitution-amendment-proposal.md).
- **Item 1**, the retriable final step T3 without a compensating action, complies with Principle V's forward-recovery rule. Under v5.0.0 it was a documented deviation, and no deviation remains.
- **Items 2, 3, and 5** comply with explicit v5.1.0 clauses: per-attempt idempotency, settled-state consistency, and the split between end-to-end and direct compensation tests.
- **Item 4** remains a reading, cited in the Constitution Check.
- **In-process mechanisms** comply with C-II, including its v5.1.0 rule. Caches, locks, the settlement registry, and the recovery schedule accelerate or coordinate work within one process, but are never the sole authority for durability, idempotency, inventory correctness, or cross-instance consistency (R7, R19).

**Rationale**: The Governance section requires documented deviations rather than silent weakening. The amendment replaced the deviation with an explicit rule, after review and approval, and weakened no safety invariant.

| Option | Why deferred | Adopt only if |
|---|---|---|
